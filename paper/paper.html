Abstract

Contemporary welfare states are characterized by the public provision of services such as education, health care, and pensions. Such services have experienced a process of commodification over the last years, whereby access to public goods increasingly depends on individual payment capacity, linked to the concept of market justice (Lane, 1986; Lindh, 2015). In this context, the study of market justice preferences acquires relevance as support for that type of distribution undermines the core of the welfare state. Previous literature has found modest support for the role of class and status variables in shaping market justice preferences within a self-interest logic, usually from a static (cross-sectional) perspective. In the present paper, we include different measures of subjective social status, arguing that changes in this dimension operate above and beyond objective status measures. Furthermore, we add a dynamic perspective, arguing that increasing objective and subjective status over time will be associated with stronger endorsement of market-justice preferences. This study draws on data from the Chilean Longitudinal Social Survey (ELSOC), a representative panel of the Chilean urban population with six waves from 2016 to 2023 (N = 1326). This unique dataset offers, for the first time, the possibility to analyze variations across three dimensions of subjective social status: own, family of origin, and (future) offspring. The results of the multilevel longitudinal models indicate that subjective status is positively associated with market justice preferences (ceteris paribus). Moreover, the association between status variables and market justice preferences is far from being static, such that increasing status leads to stronger support for the role of individual payment capacity in accessing public goods.

Keywords: Longitudinal multilevel models, Market justice preferences, Social stratification, Social subjective status
