A3. Construction of the subjective social status trajectory

The SSS trajectory summarises, for each respondent \(i\) and wave \(t\), the direction of the perceived status path across three generations. Respondents rate the status of their family of origin (\(S^{O}_{it}\)), their own current status (\(S^{P}_{it}\)) and the status they expect their children to attain (\(S^{C}_{it}\)), each on a 0-10 scale. These ratings are placed on a generational axis \(g \in \{1, 2, 3\}\) (origin, own, children), and the trajectory is defined as the ordinary least squares slope of status on \(g\), estimated separately for each person-wave with complete ratings (\(n\) = 7,791 person-waves):

\[ S_{igt} = \alpha_{it} + \beta_{it}\, g + e_{igt}, \qquad g = 1, 2, 3. \]

Closed form. With three equally spaced points, the slope has a closed form. Since \(\bar{g} = 2\), the deviations \(g - \bar{g}\) are \(-1\), \(0\) and \(1\), and

\[ \hat{\beta}_{it} = \frac{\sum_{g} (g - \bar{g})\, S_{igt}}{\sum_{g} (g - \bar{g})^2} = \frac{S^{C}_{it} - S^{O}_{it}}{2}. \]

Each rating contributes to the slope in proportion to its distance from the centre of the generational axis (Table 11). Own status lies at the centre and therefore receives a weight of zero.

Geometric interpretation. An OLS line passes through the mean point \((\bar{g}, \bar{S})\). Because own status is located at \(\bar{g}\), changing it shifts the fitted line vertically but does not alter its slope. Figure 1 illustrates this with three hypothetical respondents who share the same origin (3) and expectation for their children (7) but report own status of 1, 5 and 9: the fitted lines differ in level but all have a slope of 2. Own status therefore contributes to the level of the path, which our models capture through the SSS index, and to the deviation of the path from linearity, but not to its slope. A non-zero weight for own status would require placing the three generations at unequal distances on the axis, for which the data provide no basis.

Empirical verification. In the data, the estimated slope equals \((S^{C}_{it} - S^{O}_{it})/2\) for all person-waves (maximum absolute difference: 3.6e-15), and its correlation with expected mobility relative to the family of origin (\(S^{C} - S^{O}\)) is 1.000.

Implications for the models. The trajectory is thus a rescaled version of expected mobility relative to the family of origin. When both measures are decomposed into within- and between-person components, the corresponding models are statistically equivalent: Model 5 in Table 2 reproduces the fit of Model 4 (BIC = 15982.8 and 15982.8, respectively), and its within- and between-person coefficients are 2.00 and 2.00 times those of Model 4.

Curvature. To incorporate own status into the description of the trajectory, we also estimated a quadratic specification with the generational axis centred on own status (\(g^{*} = -1, 0, 1\)):

\[ S_{igt} = \alpha_{it} + \beta_{it}\, g^{*} + \gamma_{it}\, g^{*2}, \qquad \hat{\gamma}_{it} = \frac{S^{O}_{it} - 2\,S^{P}_{it} + S^{C}_{it}}{2}. \]

Centring makes the linear and quadratic terms orthogonal: with \(g^{*} = (-1, 0, 1)\) and \(g^{*2} = (1, 0, 1)\), \(\sum g^{*}\,(g^{*2} - \overline{g^{*2}}) = \sum g^{*3} - \overline{g^{*2}} \sum g^{*} = 0\). The estimated slope \(\hat{\beta}_{it}\) is therefore identical in the linear and quadratic fits for every person-wave. Without centring (\(g = 1, 2, 3\)), \(g\) and \(g^2\) are nearly collinear and the linear coefficient changes when the quadratic term is added. The curvature indicates whether own status lies below (\(\hat{\gamma}_{it} > 0\), convex path) or above (\(\hat{\gamma}_{it} < 0\), concave path) the line connecting origin and children. In Figure 1, the respondent whose own status lies below the line has a curvature of 4, the one on the line a curvature of 0, and the one above it a curvature of -4, while all three share a slope of 2. Slope and curvature are nearly uncorrelated in the data (\(r\) = 0.07).

Table 12 compares the linear and quadratic specifications of the trajectory. Following the usual practice for polynomial terms, the quadratic model retains the slope and adds the within- and between-person components of the curvature; the first column reproduces Model 5 in Table 2. The linear model is

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{MJP}_{it} = {} & \pi_{0} + \pi_{1}\,\text{wave}_{t} + \pi_{2}\,\text{wave}_{t}^{2} + \boldsymbol{\delta}'\mathbf{X}_{it} + \lambda_{W}\,(\text{SSS}_{it} - \overline{\text{SSS}}_{i}) + \lambda_{B}\,\overline{\text{SSS}}_{i} \\ & + \theta_{W}\,(\hat{\beta}_{it} - \bar{\hat{\beta}}_{i}) + \theta_{B}\,\bar{\hat{\beta}}_{i} + u_{0i} + u_{1i}\,\text{wave}_{t} + \varepsilon_{it}, \end{aligned} \]

and the quadratic model adds the within- and between-person components of the curvature:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{MJP}_{it} = {} & \pi_{0} + \pi_{1}\,\text{wave}_{t} + \pi_{2}\,\text{wave}_{t}^{2} + \boldsymbol{\delta}'\mathbf{X}_{it} + \lambda_{W}\,(\text{SSS}_{it} - \overline{\text{SSS}}_{i}) + \lambda_{B}\,\overline{\text{SSS}}_{i} \\ & + \theta_{W}\,(\hat{\beta}_{it} - \bar{\hat{\beta}}_{i}) + \theta_{B}\,\bar{\hat{\beta}}_{i} + \phi_{W}\,(\hat{\gamma}_{it} - \bar{\hat{\gamma}}_{i}) + \phi_{B}\,\bar{\hat{\gamma}}_{i} + u_{0i} + u_{1i}\,\text{wave}_{t} + \varepsilon_{it}, \end{aligned} \]

where \(\text{MJP}_{it}\) is the market justice preferences index of respondent \(i\) in wave \(t\); \(\mathbf{X}_{it}\) contains political ideology, sex, age, membership in the top income decile and university education (both between-person); \(\text{SSS}_{it}\) is the SSS index (the mean of the three ratings); \(\hat{\beta}_{it}\) and \(\hat{\gamma}_{it}\) are the trajectory slope and curvature defined above; bars denote person means across waves; \(u_{0i}\) and \(u_{1i}\) are a person-specific random intercept and random wave slope; and \(\varepsilon_{it}\) is the residual. The subscripts \(W\) and \(B\) mark within- and between-person coefficients. Adding the curvature does not improve fit (BIC = 15998.2, compared with 15982.8), and neither of its components is statistically significant. The curvature is therefore not included in the main tables.

The slope coefficients differ slightly between the two models (within: -0.027 and -0.026; between: -0.026 and -0.028). This is not a change in the trajectory measure itself: for each person-wave, the estimated slope is identical in the linear and quadratic fits, because centring the generational axis makes the linear and quadratic terms orthogonal. The difference arises in the multilevel model, where each coefficient is estimated net of the other predictors. Within persons, the curvature is weakly correlated with the slope (\(r\) = 0.13) and with the SSS index (\(r\) = 0.24); between persons, the corresponding correlations are -0.05 and 0.18. Adding the curvature therefore slightly reallocates shared variance, and the resulting changes in the slope coefficients are small relative to their standard errors.
